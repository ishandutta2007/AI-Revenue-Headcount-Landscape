## 📌 Executive Summary

The generative AI and machine learning landscape has evolved into distinct structural layers—from foundational silicon and physical data center facilities up to cloud infrastructure, LLM model providers, and autonomous enterprise AI agents. 

This repository tracks market sizing, employment dynamics, and capital/labor productivity metrics across the core verticals of the artificial intelligence ecosystem.

## 💡 Sector Breakdown & Taxonomy

### 1. AI Agents (Autonomous Applications)
* **Scope**: Domain-specific workflow agents, autonomous coding assistants, robotic process automation (RPA) integrations, customer service bots, and AI-first SaaS solutions.
* **Workforce Profile**: Broad, labor-intensive deployment and customer engineering ecosystems with distributed operations worldwide.

### 2. Foundation Models: API vs. Non-API
* **Foundation Models (API - Token & Cloud Inference)**: Independent frontier labs and model vendors monetizing directly via developer APIs, raw token throughput, and hosted model gateways.
* **Foundation Models (Non-API - Applications & Enterprise Seats)**: Frontier labs monetizing via packaged enterprise subscriptions, seat-based coding tools, consumer Pro/Plus memberships, and dedicated enterprise deployments. Demonstrates extreme operating leverage and ultra-high revenue per employee.

### 3. AI Cloud Infrastructure (AI IaaS)
* **Scope**: GPU cloud clusters, managed training/fine-tuning clusters, serverless inference hosting, distributed ML orchestration, and specialized AI cloud service providers (Hyperscalers & Neoclouds).

### 4. AI Compute & Silicon Hardware
* **Dedicated AI Accelerators**: Pure-play GPUs, TPUs, and specialized tensor accelerator chips purpose-built for heavy LLM training and high-throughput inference workloads.
* **Broader AI Chipsets**: Edge NPUs, server CPUs optimized for AI workloads, custom hyperscaler ASICs, and High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM3e/HBM4) supply chains.

### 5. AI Data Centers & Energy Infrastructure
* **Scope**: Physical hyperscale facilities, modular data halls, liquid cooling systems, power distribution units (PDUs), grid interconnects, and clean power infrastructure supporting high-density AI clusters.

## 🔍 Key Insights & Industry Trends

* **Extreme Capital Leverage in Frontier Labs:** Non-API foundation model providers generate an estimated ~$10M per employee in 2026, highlighting unprecedented software operating leverage driven by automated workflows and lean team structures.
* **Physical Infrastructure Dominates Top-Line Spend:** AI Data Centers ($180.60B in 2026E) and AI IaaS ($102.97B in 2026E) represent the largest monetary segments, reflecting massive hyperscaler capital expenditures (CapEx) to meet training and inference compute demands.
* **High Employment Distribution in Agent Applications:** The AI Agents vertical represents over 1.1 million jobs, driven by implementation consultancies, integration engineering, and vertical solutions built on top of underlying foundation models.

## 📝 Methodology & Notes

* **Foundation Models (API vs. Non-API):** API tracks standalone token licensing and model endpoint usage; Non-API captures enterprise seat licenses, IDE integrations, and consumer subscriptions across frontier labs.
* **AI Compute (Dedicated vs. Broader):** Dedicated covers pure-play AI accelerators and GPUs; Broader includes AI-capable CPUs, edge NPUs, custom ASICs, and High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM).
* **AI Data Centers:** Headcount reflects direct operational and specialized facility roles added during the 2025–2026 infrastructure expansion, excluding indirect construction and civil supply-chain workers.
* **Revenue Per Employee:** Calculated strictly as:
  $$\text{Revenue Per Employee (2026)} = \frac{\text{2026 Estimated Revenue}}{\text{Jobs Created Till Now}}$$
